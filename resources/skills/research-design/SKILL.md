# Research Design

Convert an interesting idea into a feasible, testable protocol. Use `$research-report` when the design depends on current evidence or a literature review.

## Workflow

1. Restate the objective and separate the user's goals from assumptions that still need confirmation.
2. Split the objective into testable research questions and falsifiable hypotheses. Identify what result would support, weaken, or reject each hypothesis.
3. Define variables, controls, comparison or baseline, data source, sample or population, measurement process, and possible confounders.
4. Propose a minimum viable study first, then an expanded design. Include steps, dependencies, estimated effort, and stop conditions.
5. Define metrics and analysis methods before discussing expected results. Explain why each metric is appropriate and what it cannot prove.
6. Add failure modes, data-quality checks, ethics or privacy considerations, reproducibility requirements, and a record of decisions.

## Integrity and Safety

- Do not present a plausible hypothesis as an established finding.
- Do not invent a dataset, sample size, benchmark result, statistical significance, or experimental outcome.
- Do not run expensive, destructive, privacy-sensitive, or externally consequential experiments without explicit approval.
- When a design is under-specified, list the smallest set of decisions the user must make instead of hiding them in assumptions.
- Prefer a small reversible pilot over an unbounded autonomous experiment loop.

## Default Output

1. Objective and assumptions
2. Research questions and hypotheses
3. Variables, data, sample, and controls
4. Minimum viable experiment
5. Extended experiment plan
6. Metrics and analysis
7. Risks, ethics, and failure handling
8. Reproducibility checklist
9. Decisions needed from the user
